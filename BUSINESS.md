## What TMC actually is

**TMC Freeriderz Pro Shop** — a freeride/freeski specialty shop in Whistler, BC, Canada. TMC stands for **"True Movement Core."**

Initial assumption going into this project (that "Freeriderz" implied mountain biking) was wrong — confirmed both from the live Shopify catalog and the site itself. This is a **ski shop** first, with a distinctive secondary identity as a mountain-town youth-culture shop: skateboards, guitars, vinyl records, and jewelry alongside the ski gear. That mix isn't incoherent inventory — it reflects the founder's own framing of TMC as "freeskiing culture blended with music and street culture."

## History & founding story

This is the richest material for an About/story section — real, specific, and emotionally warm, not generic shop copy.

- Founded **1994** by **Yosuke "Yo-ski" Henry Hamazaki**, at age 16, in the upstairs storage space of his parents' shop, **Whistler Gifts & Sports**. Originally called **"The Mogul Corner."**
- Yosuke was a competitive mogul skier, coached by **Mike Douglas** — later dubbed "the Godfather of Freeskiing." Yosuke and **JP Auclair** got early Oakley sponsorship together via Jean-Luc Brassard at summer camps.
- Independently verified via press (Skiing History, Pique Newsmagazine, The Globe and Mail): Mike Douglas, JP Auclair, JF Cusson, Vincent Dorion and others — the **"New Canadian Air Force"** — invented modern freeskiing at Whistler/Blackcomb in the mid-90s, training on the Horstman Glacier and borrowing tricks from snowboarding terrain parks. TMC's own history page positions the shop as directly embedded in that founding moment of the sport — same town, same era, same social circle.
- The **Hamazaki family** has deeper Whistler roots: per the Whistler Chamber of Commerce, the family ran a **heli-skiing operation** and retail shops in Whistler from the **late 1970s**, and were influential in opening the **Japanese ski-tourism market** to Whistler. The founder is explicitly framed as a "second-generation" Whistler entrepreneur.
- **2019**: 25th anniversary (covered by Pique Newsmagazine — article found but blocked from direct fetch, headline only, not independently confirmed content).
- **This season (2026) is the 30th anniversary** — celebrated with limited-edition Armada & Faction collaboration skis featuring the founder's own artwork on the topsheet. This is a live, timely moment worth surfacing prominently in the new site.
- **Logo (adopted 1997)**: a tribal-style design drawing on sacred art and linework from various Indigenous cultures, explicitly honoring the **Sk̲wx̲wú7mesh (Squamish) and L̓il̓wat7úl (Líl'wat)** Nations, on whose traditional territory Whistler sits. This carries real cultural weight, not decoration. **Client context (from Toby, who has raised this with the founder before): the founder likely isn't open to changing the logo itself.** The workable direction is improving production quality (a cleaner/higher-res vector redraw of the existing mark, not a redesign) and finding/creating tidy variations for different uses (favicon, mono/reversed versions, etc.) — not reinterpreting the design. Don't propose a redesign without Toby raising it first.
- Founder's own framing (paraphrased from his staff bio): TMC represents "pure freeskiing culture blended with music and street culture, rejecting mainstream appeal in favor of individual rider identity and style" — this is the throughline that explains skis + skateboards + guitars + vinyl coexisting under one roof.
- Sources: https://tmcfreeriderz.com/pages/history-of-tmc, https://www.whistlerchamber.com/second-generation/, https://www.skiinghistory.org/article/mike-douglas-godfather-freeskiing, https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/national/canada-150/how-canadians-at-whistler-invented-the-future-ofskiing/article33820021/

## Positioning in Whistler

- Self-described (and externally corroborated) as **"North America's first freeski-specific/freeride pro shop."**
- Known specifically for **custom ski/boot tuning and fitting** — "one of the most highly regarded [tuning shops] in Whistler," 30+ years experience, locals bring personal gear for mid-season tunes — plus **ski demos**, and functioning as a **sponsored-athlete hub**. Positioned as a locals'/serious-skier destination and gathering spot, not primarily a tourist rental counter.
- Runs a **freeride ski academy** (coaching), consistent with the founder's own mogul-coaching background.
- Two side eco-products created under the TMC umbrella: **"Whistler's Lips"** (organic mountain-flavored lip balm) and **"Chocowax"** (biodegradable ski wax) — a genuine environmental/innovation streak worth reflecting in brand voice somewhere, beyond "just a ski shop."
- **In-house apparel line** ("TMC Freeriderz Collection"): OG Puff Tee ($49 CAD), TMC OG Riderz Hoodie ($125 CAD), TMC Yoski T, TMC PipeGirl T, beanies, trucker caps, posters — vintage-inspired graphics. A real merch/apparel brand in its own right, not just third-party retail.
- Also stocks **Juzzi Studios**, a clothing line founded 2019/2020 by current staff member Izzie Larter — matches the "Juzzi Studios - Elements Collection" seen live in the Shopify catalog.
- **Team/athlete roster** (site nav): Grete Eliassen, Aidan Mulvihill, Anders Ujejski, Chase Ujejski, Chris Turpin, Dana Flahr, Dean Bercovitch, Jordan Clarke, Kai Smart, Luke Smart, Riley Culver, Simon D'artois, Warren Williams, **Yuki Tsubota**. Several are genuinely notable: Grete Eliassen (well-known pro freeskier), Simon D'artois and Yuki Tsubota (Olympic freestyle skiers). Yuki Tsubota's bio: coached by Yosuke from a young age, TMC still tunes her equipment — *"We continue to take pride in tuning her equipment, and giving her anything that she may need to be the best."* Strong mentor/family brand voice.
- **Brands carried**: Armada (heavily — ARW/ARV/Signature/Zero series, an early/pioneering stockist), Faction, K2, SPY, Marker, Look.
- **Staff**: includes **Toby King** — "Ski Gear Sales & Online," UK web-developer background, relocated to Whistler in 2023 (i.e. the person running this project is TMC staff). Also Izzie Larter (Sales & Tech, ski technician, Whistler Blackcomb instructor supervisor, Juzzi Studios founder).

## Raw material for brand/visual design

- **"TMC" = True Movement Core"** — a genuinely usable tagline/wordmark-adjacent phrase.
- Recurring vocabulary: "gathering place," "freeride/freeski," "core," "shred," "pow," "OG," the ski + skate + music/vinyl + street culture mashup.
- 30th-anniversary framing (current season) is a live, timely brand moment worth surfacing prominently on the homepage.
- Strong "mentor/family" emotional throughline — Olympic athletes personally coached by the founder since childhood, second-generation Whistler family business, honest non-upsell reputation — strong material for an About/story section with real emotional weight rather than generic stock copy.
- No existing brand color palette to preserve — this is a genuinely open decision, informed by the above (freeride/mountain culture, 90s-rooted, music/street-culture cross-over, Whistler setting) rather than constrained by legacy branding.
- The 1997 logo's Indigenous-art influence should inform the visual language with real respect — this isn't a "cool graphic," it's a specific cultural acknowledgment the shop has carried for almost 30 years. Scope for this project: quality/production improvements and tidy variations of the existing mark, not a redesign (see History section above).
